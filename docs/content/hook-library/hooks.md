# Hook library: the "real Iron Man suit" niche

Pulled 25 Oct 2026 from the YouTube Data API. Three videos that beat their own channel's normal views by a wide margin, what their hooks and formats look like, and reusable templates for Project Halcyon.

## How "beat the channel's usual views" was measured

- **Usual views** = the median views of the channel's 50 most recent uploads, leaving out the video itself.
- **Outlier score** = the video's views divided by that median. 10× means ten times what the channel normally gets.
- 15 niche searches (real builds, jet suits, exoskeletons, arc reactor and Iron Man physics), each sorted by relevance and by views, for videos uploaded since January 2022: 397 videos from 278 channels.
- Sorted into real builds, physics explainers, costume builds and movie clips; the movie clips and unrelated videos were dropped, leaving 275. About 50 of the strongest (every long video with at least 40,000 views, plus a hand-picked set of Shorts) were checked against their channels' medians.
- **Limits:** views depend on a video's age, Shorts get far more feed distribution than long videos, and the search only reaches what YouTube search returns for these terms.

## H1. "Why we can't build an iron man suit in real life"

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Link | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zXr--BjUqUA |
| Channel | Get Around How, 6,540 subscribers |
| Published | 26 Mar 2023, 9 min 29 s |
| Views | 648,141 against a channel median of 366: **1,768×** |
| Engagement | 14K likes (2.3%), 1,461 comments |
| Format | Reality check, one Iron Man feature at a time, each ending in a verdict |

**Structure (from the chapters):** Intro 0:00 → AI 0:45 → Flight 2:02 → Repulsor 3:29 → Durability 4:40 → Strength 5:48 → Arc Reactor 6:53 → Verdict 8:37.

**The opening, beat by beat (0:00 to 0:42):**

1. **0:00 A wish everyone shares.** Almost nobody watches Iron Man without wanting the suit.
2. **0:08 A reframe.** Tony's superpower is engineering; he wasn't born with powers, he built them.
3. **0:17 The question.** With today's best scientists and unlimited money, how likely is a real suit?
4. **0:24 The underdog twist.** If one man did it in a cave, surely a lab can; this sets up the title's "can't".
5. **0:33 Channel plug and roadmap.** Subscribe, then "are we close, or is it stuck in a comic forever?"

**Each section after that:** the movie's claim, then the nearest real thing (a named jet suit, a DARPA AI fighter-jet programme), then a one-line verdict ("on our way but far").

**Why it probably worked (my inference):** the title is a contrarian claim on a topic people already search for; the chapter list promises a tour they can skim; every section is self-contained; the comment section shows meme energy ("a box of scraps"), which drives replies.

**Take for Halcyon:** the system-by-system verdict structure and the closing scoreboard. Replace generic analogies with your own measured numbers.
**Avoid:** about 40 seconds of set-up and a subscribe plug before any information.

## H2. "How the Arc Reactor Works"

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Link | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DZxsau1mAYc |
| Channel | Dianax, 92,800 subscribers |
| Published | 9 Oct 2026, 3 min 16 s |
| Views | 883,759 against a channel median of 7,250: **122×** |
| Engagement | 1.2% likes, 352 comments |
| Format | Short explainer that leads with a size-versus-power contradiction |

**Structure (from the chapters):** The Arc Reactor in action 0:00 → What's inside the Arc Reactor 0:24 → Why we can't build one (yet) 2:06 → Behind the scenes 2:51.

**The hook (from the description; the spoken opening was not read):**

1. A question that sets size against power: what would it take to power an Iron Man suit from something the size of your palm?
2. A promise of real physics.
3. A startling comparison: the output of two nuclear reactors.

**Pattern:** spectacle first (24 s of the reactor "in action"), then the mechanism, then an honest "can't, yet" chapter, then a behind-the-scenes finish.

**Why it probably worked (inference):** it is the same topic as your Short 1, only two weeks old, and the contradiction fits in one sentence.

**Take for Halcyon:** pair a small object with a huge number in the first line; keep a chapter literally titled "why we can't build it (yet)"; end with how you checked.
**Avoid:** copying the "two reactors" comparison. Use your own figure: 3 GW in the film, 300 MW of waste heat.

## H3. "Russian engineer just solved the one problem stopping anyone from building a real Iron Man suit"

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Links | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m0OK7eU8DiU and https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6d4n-89evvU |
| Channel | Nic Conley, 40,000 subscribers |
| Published | 16 Oct 2025 and 11 Jan 2026, both 1 minute Shorts |
| Views | 15,896,834 and 8,500,540 against a channel median of 2,973: **5,346× and 2,859×** |
| Engagement | 2.7% and 2.9% likes; 2,361 and 1,837 comments |
| Format | Open-loop Short: one named person, one problem, a claim that it is solved |

**Pattern:** [a specific person] + "just solved" + "the one problem stopping anyone from" + [the big outcome]. The title promises a single problem and its answer, and the viewer has to watch to learn which problem it is. The second upload's description repeats the title and then states "the biggest problem" as armour that looks movie-perfect and also protects.

**The topic has proof beyond one channel.** Three more Shorts about the same engineer's real suit also did far better than their channels' norm: two from Cinenion (12.9M and 12.3M views, about 180× its median) and one from Bhanu Rai (14.9M, 486×). The engineer's own 17-minute build documentary on ALEX LAB reached 7.1M views (43×). In autumn 2025 "real-life Iron Man suit" was a topic with large demand.

**Why it probably worked (inference):** a human protagonist, a tangible object, a single curiosity gap, and a topic with built-in demand.

**Take for Halcyon:** the open-loop title and the single-problem framing. Your honest version: the one problem is not the reactor but hovering (see Script C).
**Avoid:** two things. These Shorts re-use another creator's footage, which risks copyright claims. And "solved" is a claim you can only make if you can back it, so say what you actually calculated.

| 4 | **Grade it system by system**, finishing on a scoreboard | H1 | "I graded Iron Man's suit with real numbers: 5 of 6 are possible" |

## Honest caveats

- Big view counts are not proof of quality: some of these are clickbait or re-uploads, and the outlier score says nothing about whether viewers followed or returned.
- Three videos is a pattern, not a law. Test any template with a Short before building a long video on it.
- Recheck the numbers every few months; a hit from 2023 may not reflect what works now.
