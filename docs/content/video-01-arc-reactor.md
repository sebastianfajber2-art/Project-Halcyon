## 0:00 Hook (15 s)

**On camera:** "I want to build a real Iron Man suit. Not a costume: one that flies. So the first thing I needed was an arc reactor. Problem: physics says it can't exist. Here's why, and what I'm using instead."

**On screen:** quick cuts of the suit drawing, the Fusion model spinning, the endurance chart.

## 0:15 What the arc reactor would have to do (60 s)

**Say:** A palm-sized device putting out gigawatts. Let's test that against one rule: no machine is 100% efficient. Even at 90%, three gigawatts means 300 megawatts of waste heat in your chest. A person can shed a few hundred watts.

**On screen:** "3 GW × 10% loss = 300,000,000 W of heat" vs "your body: ~300 W".

## 1:15 Can fusion fit in your chest? (90 s)

**Say:** Real fusion is being built right now, by Commonwealth Fusion, Helion and TAE. But a plasma has to be hot, dense and held long enough (the Lawson criterion), and small plasmas leak heat faster than they make it. That's why the smallest serious designs are metres across, not centimetres. And the "clean" fuel people like for Iron Man, proton-boron, needs around a billion degrees and loses most of its energy as X-rays.

**On screen:** a person next to a room-sized fusion device silhouette.

## 2:45 What about a tiny nuclear battery? (90 s)

**Say:** NASA powers Mars rovers with plutonium-238. It gives 0.57 watts of heat per gram. A chest-sized one makes about 70 watts of electricity. Enough for electronics; flying needs around 500,000 watts. And a real reactor big enough to fly needs shielding: in the 1950s a nuclear test bomber carried an 11-tonne shield just for the crew.

**On screen:** "Pu-238 battery: ~70 W. Flight: ~500,000 W."

## 4:15 The real problem is hovering (90 s)

**Say:** Hovering means pushing your whole weight down through the air every second. For my suit that's about 2,000 newtons, and it burns about 5 kilograms of fuel a minute. A wing changes everything: in forward flight the air holds you up, and you need only 400 newtons.

**On screen:** the endurance chart from the design doc; highlight "hover: 305 kg/h" vs "cruise: 57 kg/h".

## 5:45 So what powers Project Halcyon? (90 s)

**Say:** Jet fuel. Kerosene holds about 10 to 20 times more usable energy per kilo than batteries. Six small jet turbines, 2,440 newtons of thrust, and a carbon wing that swings between speed, turn and stop modes. Simulation says about 24 minutes from a vertical takeoff and over an hour from a glider-style winch launch.

**On screen:** top-view drawing, wing modes, sim output.

## 7:15 Honest part: this is dangerous (45 s)

**Say:** People have died flying jet wings, including professional pilots. So nothing goes on my body until it passes simulation, bench tests, dummy tests and unmanned flights. That's the plan, and you'll see every step, including the failures.
